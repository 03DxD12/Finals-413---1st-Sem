🎓 1. Education, Training & Certifications
Awareness

Short messages making everyone notice security.
Example: Staff reminder about phishing texts.

Training

Teaching specific hands-on skills for a job.
Example: Lab course on restoring server backups.

Education

Long-term learning building broad understanding and judgment (e.g., IT/Cybersecurity Degree program).

💡 Memory Aid: Awareness tells you that, Training teaches you how, Education helps you understand why.

NIST SP 800-50 Rev. 1 (2024) Cycle

A continuous cycle: Plan → Deliver → Measure Results → Improve.
Key Rule: Completing a module is not proof that behavior changed!

Self-Study & Ethics

Practice strictly on systems you own or sandboxes (OWASP Juice Shop, WebGoat, official CTFs). Unauthorized testing is illegal even with good intentions; verbal permission is never enough.

Certifications & Career Roadmap

• Vendor-Neutral: Universal concepts across vendors (CompTIA Security+, ISC2 CISSP/SSCP/CC, ISACA CISA/CISM, GIAC).
• Vendor-Specific: One vendor's technologies (Fortinet NSE, Cisco, AWS, Microsoft).
• Associate of ISC2: Earned by passing exams before completing the 5-year experience requirement.
• Maintenance: Requires annual Continuing Professional Education (CPE) credits and fees.
• Roadmap: Degree → Entry vendor-neutral → Practitioner/vendor → Advanced/Management.

Cyber Workforce Frameworks

• DoD Manual 8140.03 (Feb 2023): Replaced DoD 8570.01-M. Ties qualification directly to Cyber Workforce roles rather than only certs.
• NICE Framework (NIST SP 800-181): Universal language for cyber roles, tasks, knowledge, and skills.

⚖️ 2. Ethics, Law & Professional Codes

Law versus Ethics

• Law: Government statutes enforced by courts (fines or jail).
• Ethics: Professional values and codes (loss of trust, job, credentials).
• Crucial: An action can be legal but unethical (e.g., viewing celebrity records out of curiosity without clinical need).

ISC2 Code of Ethics (Strict Priority Order)

If duties conflict, the earlier canon always comes first:

  1. Protect society, the common good, and public trust.
  2. Act honorably, honestly, justly, responsibly, and legally.
  3. Provide diligent and competent service.
  4. Advance and protect the profession.

Workplace Ethical Actions

• Responsible Disclosure: Stop immediately, refrain from taking data, report privately to owner. Never exploit, publicly post, or demand money.
• Conflict of Interest: Disclose promptly and step away (recusal).
• Improper Instructions: Refuse unethical requests (e.g., hiding logs from auditors) and escalate through proper channels.

📜 3. Regulatory Compliance Laws
⚠️ Compliance ≠ Security: Compliance is a minimum baseline. Passing an audit does not guarantee an organization won't be breached.
Rule Covers Protects Core Requirement
FISMA U.S. Federal agencies & contractors Federal systems Risk-based continuous monitoring (NIST SP 800-37, 800-53).
HIPAA Healthcare plans & providers PHI Admin, Physical & Technical safeguards; Privacy & Breach rules.
GLBA Financial institutions NPI Privacy notices & written security Safeguards Rule.
SOX U.S. public corporations Financial reporting Section 302: Executive certification of accurate financial controls.
FERPA Schools receiving federal funds Student education records Requires signed written student consent before records release.
CIPA Schools/libraries on E-Rate Minors online Internet safety policy & content filtering against harmful material.
PCI DSS Entities processing card data Cardholder data Contractual standard (not law); v4.0.1 is current. Tokenization reduces scope.
🏛️ 4. FISMA & Governance

FISMA Roles & Responsibilities

• OMB: Formulates federal cybersecurity policy.
• DHS/CISA: Directs operational protection and security directives.
• NIST: Publishes mandatory standards (RMF SP 800-37, SP 800-53 controls).
• Inspectors General (IG): Perform independent annual audits.
• Continuous Monitoring: Mandated because IT systems, configurations, and threats evolve daily.

Lessons from the 2015 OPM Breach

Over 21M records stolen. Key lessons:
1. Maintain an accurate and complete system inventory.
2. Implement strong authentication (MFA/smart cards).
3. Patch vulnerable software and systems promptly.

🇵🇭 5. Philippine Cyber Legislation

RA 10173 – Data Privacy Act of 2012

Governed by three mandatory pillars:
1. Transparency: Inform individuals why and how data is collected.
2. Legitimate Purpose: Process only for lawful, declared reasons.
3. Proportionality: Collect only the minimal necessary data.
• Enforcement: National Privacy Commission (NPC).
• Breach Notice: Report qualifying breaches within 72 hours of knowledge.

RA 10175 – Cybercrime Prevention Act of 2012

Defines criminal penalties for offenses including illegal access (hacking), illegal interception, and data interference.
