1. Professional Education, Training, and Certification
Level Meaning Real-World Example
Awareness Short messages that make everyone notice security and know what to do. A reminder to all staff about a new SMS/email phishing campaign.
Training Teaching a specific, hands-on skill for a specific job role. A hands-on course on restoring enterprise servers from backup.
Education Long-term academic learning that builds understanding and judgment. A university degree program (e.g., BSIT, Master's in Cybersecurity).
💡 Key Memory Aid: Awareness tells you that, Training teaches you how, Education helps you understand why.

NIST SP 800-50 Rev. 1 (2024) Learning Cycle

A cybersecurity learning program is an ongoing continuous cycle: Plan → Deliver → Measure Results → Improve.
Critical reminder: Completing a training module is not proof that user behavior has actually changed.

Self-Study & Ethical Practice Rules

Practice strictly on systems you own or authorized training environments (e.g., OWASP Juice Shop, WebGoat, official CTFs). Unauthorized testing is illegal even with good intentions, and verbal permission is never legally sufficient.

Certification Type Core Focus Leading Examples
Vendor-Neutral Fundamental concepts that apply across many products and platforms. CompTIA Security+, ISC2 (CISSP, SSCP, CC), ISACA (CISA, CISM), GIAC.
Vendor-Specific Hands-on operational skills for one company's specific proprietary products. Fortinet NSE, Cisco (CCNA/CCNP), Microsoft Azure Security, AWS Security.

Credential Maintenance & Roadmap

• CPEs: Credentials require Continuing Professional Education (CPE) credits and annual maintenance fees to stay active.
• Associate of ISC2: A candidate who passes an ISC2 exam without the required 5 years of verified experience becomes an Associate of ISC2 while earning it.
• Recommended Career Roadmap: BSIT Degree → Entry-level vendor-neutral (CC/Security+) → Practitioner/vendor-specific (SSCP/NSE) → Advanced/Management (CISSP/CISM). Reviewed annually.

Cybersecurity Workforce Frameworks

• DoD Manual 8140.03 (Feb 2023): Replaced DoD 8570.01-M. Ties baseline qualification requirements directly to defined work roles in the DoD Cyber Workforce Framework rather than relying solely on certifications.
• NICE Workforce Framework (NIST SP 800-181): Standard common language for cybersecurity roles, tasks, knowledge, and skills for job descriptions and career planning.
• Fortinet Micro-Credentials: Always follow the instructor's latest syllabus; never share accounts or exam answers.

2. Ethics, Compliance, and Laws

Law versus Ethics

• Law: Written by government, enforced by courts; penalties include monetary fines or imprisonment.
• Ethics: Moral values and professional codes of conduct; penalties include loss of trust, employment termination, or revocation of credentials.
• Key Principle: An action can be legal but unethical (e.g., browsing a celebrity's medical records out of curiosity without clinical need). Professional codes often expect higher conduct than the law.

ISC2 Code of Ethics (Strict Priority Order)

  1. Protect society, the common good, and public trust.
  2. Act honorably, honestly, justly, responsibly, and legally.
  3. Provide diligent and competent service.
  4. Advance and protect the profession.

Hierarchy Rule: If ethical duties conflict, the earlier canon always comes first.

Professional Ethical Conduct

• Responsible Disclosure: Upon noticing a security flaw, stop immediately, refrain from downloading or copying data, and report the vulnerability privately to the owner. Never exploit, publicly post, or demand money.
• Conflict of Interest: Fully disclose any conflict and step away from the decision (recusal).
• Improper Instructions: Explicitly refuse unethical orders (e.g., hiding incident logs from auditors) and escalate through proper reporting channels.

⚠️ Compliance ≠ Security: Compliance is a minimum legal/contractual baseline and proof of adherence. An organization can pass every regulatory audit and still suffer a catastrophic security breach!

3. FISMA & Philippine Cyber Legislation

FISMA Governance & OPM Breach Lessons

• Passed in 2002 and modernized in 2014. Applies to federal agencies and their IT contractors.
• Governance Roles: OMB sets overarching policy; DHS/CISA oversees operations; NIST authors standards (SP 800-37 RMF, SP 800-53 controls); Inspectors General conduct independent audits; agencies report yearly.
• Continuous Monitoring: FISMA mandates continuous monitoring because systems and cyber threats evolve daily.
• 2015 OPM Breach Lessons: Maintain a 100% complete system inventory, enforce strong authentication (MFA/smart cards), and patch vulnerabilities on time.

Philippine Laws (RA 10173 & RA 10175)

• RA 10173 (Data Privacy Act of 2012): Governs personal information processing based on three mandatory pillars:
  1. Transparency: Inform data subjects why and how their data is gathered.
  2. Legitimate Purpose: Process data only for lawful, declared reasons.
  3. Proportionality: Collect only the minimal necessary data.
  Enforcement: National Privacy Commission (NPC). Security breaches must generally be reported within 72 hours of knowledge.
• RA 10175 (Cybercrime Prevention Act of 2012): Establishes criminal penalties for offenses like illegal access, illegal interception, and data interference.
